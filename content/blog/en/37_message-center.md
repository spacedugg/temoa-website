# Amazon Message Center: using buyer communication without breaking the rules

Communication with buyers on Amazon runs through one regulated channel: buyer-seller messaging, usually called the Message Center. Used well it helps you solve problems, avoid returns and build trust. Used badly it puts you in breach of Amazon's strict contact policies. This article explains how to find the Message Center, use it professionally and stay inside the rules.

## What the Message Center is

The Message Center is the official channel through which sellers and buyers exchange messages on Amazon, about orders, questions or problems. Amazon deliberately mediates this communication to protect buyers and keep the exchange traceable. As a seller you find incoming messages in the buyer communication area of your account. Buyers see them in their account and usually by email as well.

## What you may use it for

Amazon permits communication that serves the fulfilment of an order or customer service. Typical permitted purposes: clarifying a delivery address or product variant, resolving a problem with an order, answering a customer question, or exchanging information necessary to complete the order. The guiding principle: the message has to serve the buyer and the specific order, not your marketing.

## What is forbidden

Amazon is strict here, and breaches can lead to warnings or suspension. Prohibited among other things:

- **Promotional and marketing messages:** no offers, discounts or cross-selling.
- **Manipulative review requests:** you may not ask for *positive* reviews, offer anything in return for a review, or pressure customers.
- **Pulling customers off Amazon:** no external links to continue the purchase or the conversation elsewhere.
- **Unnecessary messages:** communication with no concrete connection to the order.

## Encouraging reviews within the rules

Reviews matter, and the request has to be neutral. Amazon provides official permitted routes, notably the standardised "Request a Review" function, which sends a neutral, compliant request. Use it rather than your own wording, which can easily tip into influencing the outcome. That way you gather reviews without putting the policies at risk.

## React fast and professionally

Speed and tone pay off. Amazon expects sellers to respond promptly to buyer enquiries, usually within around 24 hours, and slow responses can hurt your metrics. Answer in a friendly, factual, solution-oriented way. A quick, helpful reply often heads off a return or a negative review before it happens.

## Practical tips

Keep an eye on the inbox regularly and set up notifications where you can, so no enquiry slips through. Write recurring answers as templates, then adapt each one to the actual case. Document important exchanges in case a dispute or an A-to-Z Guarantee claim follows later. And when in doubt, check the current communication policies, since Amazon adjusts them.

## Conclusion

The Message Center is a valuable tool for service and problem solving, and a tightly regulated space. Use it strictly for order-related, helpful communication, leave out any promotion and any manipulative review request, and encourage reviews only through the official neutral function. Communicate fast, professionally and within the rules, and you build trust, avoid returns and negative reviews, and keep your account clean.
