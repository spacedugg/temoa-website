# Full service or specialist for Amazon? What fits your brand

Anyone looking for external support on Amazon faces a basic choice: one full-service partner handling everything, or several specialists for individual areas such as PPC, listings or creative? Both routes have clear strengths and weaknesses. This guide helps you decide what fits your brand, without prescribing a particular provider.

## The two models

A **full-service partner** takes on the whole Amazon operation: advertising, listing optimisation, creative, account management and often strategy and inventory advice too. You have one partner for everything.

The **specialist approach** combines several providers, each particularly deep in their field: a pure PPC team plus a listing specialist plus a creative provider.

## The strength of full service: integration

On Amazon everything is connected. Advertising works only as well as the listing it leads to, the listing converts only with good creative, and availability affects both. That is exactly where full service is strong. One partner sees the whole picture, can align advertising, listing and strategy and avoid friction losses. When ACoS rises, an integrated team checks not just the bids but the listing and the conversion too.

## The strength of specialists: depth

Specialists often bring more depth in their field. A pure PPC team lives and breathes advertising and is sometimes further ahead there than the PPC function of a full-service provider. If a single area is particularly critical or demanding for you, a specialist can be the better choice there.

## The hidden cost of the specialist model

The specialist approach has one frequently underestimated drawback: coordination. Several providers have to be aligned, responsibilities overlap, and nobody feels accountable for the overall result. When the PPC team criticises the listing team's listing, you as the brand sit in the middle. That friction costs time, money and often results. Brands that keep jumping between specialists frequently spend more and grow more slowly than those who consolidate.

## When each model fits

- **Full service** fits when you want all-round support, have little time for coordination and value an aligned strategy, particularly for growing brands that do not want to manage every area themselves.
- **Specialists** fit when you already have coordination capability in house, want to outsource only a single area, or need maximum depth in one critical field while steering the rest yourself.

## The hybrid route

Many brands run a mixture: a full-service partner for the overall operation plus a specialist for one particularly demanding area. Clear demarcation of responsibilities matters then, so the advantages of both models survive and no friction arises.

## Conclusion

The choice between full service and specialists is a trade-off between integration and depth. Full service scores on aligned strategy from one source and less coordination effort. Specialists score on maximum depth in a single area, at the price of friction and coordination. For most growing brands an integrated solution is the more pragmatic answer, because everything on Amazon is connected, while constant switching between specialists often costs more and grows slower. What decides it is an honest assessment of how much coordination you want to do, and can do, yourself.
