# A ranking drop on Amazon: finding the cause and recovering after algorithm changes

It is one of the most unsettling moments in the Amazon business: yesterday everything was running, today visibility and revenue collapse. The first impulse is panic, and panicked reactions often make it worse. A structured approach works better: find the cause first, then act deliberately. This article shows how to analyse a ranking drop and recover systematically.

## Diagnose first, act second

A drop can have many causes, and the right reaction depends entirely on which one it is. Whoever blindly rebuilds the listing or triples the ad budget without knowing why usually makes the situation worse. The first job is always to find out what happened.

## The most common causes

Work through the possible explanations in order:

- **Technical and listing problems:** is the offer still active? Have you unintentionally lost the Buy Box, is the product stranded, was the title or an image changed or suppressed? Such problems are frequent, and the fastest to fix.
- **Availability:** were you out of stock, even briefly? A supply gap slows the flywheel and costs ranking that only recovers slowly.
- **Competition:** has a rival cut their price, advertised aggressively or launched a better listing? Then you lose relative ground without having done anything wrong.
- **Reviews:** a run of negative reviews or a drop in the star average lowers conversion and with it ranking.
- **An algorithm change:** Amazon adjusts its search and ranking logic continuously. Sometimes placements shift broadly without you having done anything.

## Algorithm change or your own problem?

A useful test: does the drop affect one product or your whole range? A single affected product points at a listing or product-specific problem. A broad drop across many products, or reports from other sellers in the same category, points at an algorithm or market shift. The shape over time helps too: a sudden crash points at a concrete event, a gradual decline at falling conversion or growing competition.

## How to recover

Once the cause is clear, you act deliberately:

- **For technical problems:** fix them at once, reactivate, get the Buy Box back, correct suppressed content. That works fastest.
- **For availability:** secure resupply and push the flywheel again with advertising, to buy back the momentum you lost.
- **For competition:** sharpen the listing and the offer, improve conversion, check price and value proposition, adjust advertising.
- **For reviews:** address the cause of the negative reviews (product or service) and encourage new ones within the rules.
- **For algorithm changes:** stay calm. Concentrate on the fundamentals, relevance, conversion, availability and reviews, because those pay into almost every version of the algorithm.

## What to avoid

Avoid hectic complete rebuilds of the listing, several changes at once (otherwise you never know what worked) and rushed price wars. Ranking recovery usually needs a little time, because the flywheel has to gather momentum again. Patience and deliberate, individually measurable steps beat frantic activity.

## In short

A ranking drop is unsettling but rarely a mystery when you go about it systematically. Diagnose first, whether technical problem, availability, competition, reviews or algorithm, and then act deliberately rather than in panic. Whoever keeps the fundamentals strong (relevance, conversion, availability, reviews) is best armed against algorithm swings and recovers fastest. Calm and method are your biggest advantage here.
