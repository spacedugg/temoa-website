# Kindle ads: how authors and publishers sell more books with Amazon advertising

For many books Amazon is the most important sales channel, and it is also where you can advertise. Kindle ads, or more precisely Amazon Advertising for books, help authors and publishers make their titles visible in a market with millions of books. This article explains how advertising for books works, what is different about it, and what to watch when you start.

## Why advertising for books is different

Books differ from ordinary products in one decisive respect: the margin. An ebook or paperback often carries only a few euros of spread, sometimes less. That means the room for advertising cost per copy sold is small. At the same time there are possibilities other products do not have, such as follow-on purchases in a series or royalties from reading programmes. Advertising for books therefore often only pays off across the lifetime value of a reader rather than on the single title.

## The most important campaign types

For books the familiar sponsored formats are essentially what is available:

- **Sponsored Products:** advertise a single title in the search results and on detail pages. The workhorse for books too.
- **Sponsored Brands:** for authors or publishers with several titles, ideal for advertising a whole series or an author profile.
- **Lockscreen ads:** a speciality of the book world, ads that appear on Kindle e-readers and in the app and reach readers directly.

## Keyword versus product targeting

For books two targeting approaches matter particularly. Through **keywords** you reach readers searching for topics, genres or comparable titles. Through **product targeting** you place your ad directly on similar or competing books, which is especially effective because readers of neighbouring titles in a genre are often exactly your audience. Advertising deliberately alongside comparable bestsellers is one of the most effective strategies in book marketing.

## Realistic expectations for ACoS

Because of the thin margin, a high ACoS is normal for books, sometimes above what would be profitable on a single sale. That is not automatically bad: when an advertised first volume of a series pulls further purchases behind it or generates reading royalties, the campaign can pay off across the total reader value. What matters is thinking in that total value rather than only in the ACoS of the single title.

## What to watch

A good cover and a convincing product description matter especially for books, because the cover is effectively the main image and decides the click-through rate. Also make sure the category and keyword assignment is clean, so your book appears in front of the right readers. And give campaigns enough time: the book market often has longer decision paths, particularly for unknown authors.

## In short

Amazon advertising is an effective instrument for books too, and it follows its own rules. The thin margin demands thinking in reader value rather than single sales, and product targeting alongside comparable titles works particularly well. Whoever brings a convincing cover, a good description and realistic ACoS expectations can buy visibility deliberately in a crowded market, and grow profitably especially with a series.
