# An ecommerce partner beyond Amazon: when a broader partner makes sense

Many brands start on Amazon and look for a pure Amazon specialist at first. As the business grows, though, a larger question tends to arise: should I concentrate on Amazon alone, or do I need a partner who thinks about my whole ecommerce business across several channels? This guide helps you decide when a broader ecommerce partner is the better choice than a pure Amazon specialist.

## Why Amazon is only one channel

Amazon is powerful, and it is one platform, with all the risks of dependency: rule changes, fee increases, the danger of suspension. Brands that rely on Amazon alone are exposed, and they give away growth on other channels. A pure Amazon partner optimises that one channel superbly and does not see the larger picture of your own shop, further marketplaces, social commerce and brand building across every touchpoint.

## What an ecommerce partner covers in addition

A broader ecommerce partner thinks across channels. Typical additional fields:

- **Your own online shop:** building and optimising direct sales through the common shop systems, with your own customer relationship and data.
- **Further marketplaces:** in Europe Kaufland, OTTO, eBay or bol.com, in the US market Walmart, plus further European Amazon marketplaces (DE, FR, NL, ES, IT), for risk spreading and growth.
- **Cross-channel marketing:** social media, search, email, including steering traffic deliberately.
- **Brand strategy across channels:** a consistent brand experience wherever the customer buys.
- **Data and retention:** owning the customer rather than renting them through Amazon.

## When the broader partner pays off

An ecommerce partner rather than a pure Amazon specialist makes sense when you want to reduce platform dependency, want to build your own shop or further channels, when your business is large enough for cross-channel strategy to add real value, or when you want to build brand value and direct customer relationships for the long run. For a brand that wants to grow on Amazon alone, a pure Amazon specialist can be the deeper choice.

## The depth vs. breadth trade-off

A similar trade-off applies here as with full service vs. specialist. A broad ecommerce partner thinks strategically across channels and is not always as deep in highly specialised Amazon detail as a pure Amazon professional. So check carefully how strong their Amazon competence actually is. Amazon has enough particularities (PPC logic, Brand Registry, FBA, the algorithm) that superficial knowledge gets expensive here.

## What to watch for

With a broader partner the same basic criteria apply as always, demonstrable results, transparency, fair contracts, ownership of accounts and data, plus two additional questions. How deep is the Amazon competence really? And how well can they genuinely integrate the channels rather than simply servicing them side by side? Ask to see cross-channel case examples.

## Conclusion

An ecommerce partner beyond Amazon pays off when you think of your business as a brand across several channels, to reduce dependency, build your own customer relationships and grow more broadly. The price of that breadth can be less specialist depth on Amazon, which is why you should examine their Amazon competence closely. For pure Amazon scaling a specialist often remains the deeper choice. For real multichannel growth an integrated ecommerce partner is the strategically smarter route. What decides it is whether your ambition ends at Amazon or reaches beyond it.
