# "Approval needed" on Amazon: getting categories and brands ungated

You find a promising product, you go to list it, and Amazon replies "Approval needed" or "You are not approved to sell this product". What looks like a dead end is usually just a hurdle you clear with the right evidence. This article explains why approvals exist, which kinds there are and how to apply successfully.

## Why approvals exist

Amazon restricts certain categories, brands and products to protect quality, safety and trademark rights. These gated areas are meant to keep substandard, counterfeit or regulated goods from being sold unchecked. For a serious brand that is more advantage than obstacle: once you are approved, you face less competition inside the protected category.

## The main kinds of approval

- **Category approval (gated categories):** some categories, typically ones with safety or quality implications, require clearance before you can sell there.
- **Brand approval (brand gating):** many brands require authorisation, for example permission from the rights holder or proof that you are authorised to sell that brand.
- **Product and compliance approval:** individual products require specific evidence such as conformity, safety or certification documents.

## Which documents you typically need

Requirements vary, but these are the ones asked for most often:

- **Invoices:** from a legitimate wholesaler or manufacturer, often above a minimum quantity and from a defined period. The details have to match your account.
- **Rights holder authorisation:** a letter of authorisation or other proof that you may sell the brand.
- **Product and safety documents:** certificates, declarations of conformity, safety data sheets, depending on the category.
- **Product images:** sometimes photos of the goods and packaging as proof of authenticity.

## How to get the application through

Care decides the outcome. Make sure every document is complete, legible and consistent: name and address on the invoices have to match your seller account, dates must not be out of range, and the quantities stated should meet the requirement. Submit exactly what is asked for, no less, and no contradictory extra paperwork. Inconsistency is the most common reason for rejection.

## Common mistakes

Many applications fail on avoidable detail: invoices from sources Amazon does not accept (retail receipts where wholesale invoices are required), address details that do not match, redacted or incomplete records, or quantities that fall short. Read the requirements closely and meet them to the letter.

## Check before you buy

Before sourcing a product, check whether it is gated and what evidence would be needed. That saves you from buying stock you are then not allowed to list. For trademark-protected products the rule is simple: without a legitimate source and authorisation, do not buy at all.

## Conclusion

"Approval needed" is rarely a genuine dead end. It is a question of the right evidence. Work out which kind of approval is required, gather complete and consistent documents, above all clean wholesale invoices and, where needed, brand authorisation, and submit precisely what was asked. Check the requirements before you buy and assemble your records carefully, and a gated category turns from an obstacle into a competitive advantage.
