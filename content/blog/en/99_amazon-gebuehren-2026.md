# Amazon fees in 2026: a portfolio calculation

There is no useful single 2026 fee for every Amazon marketplace. Category, price, dimensions, returns and payout timing determine the outcome. EU announcements and US programme changes must be assessed separately in the relevant account.

## Build two views

First, calculate unit contribution by store and ASIN: net order revenue less product, selling, fulfilment, storage, expected returns and advertising costs. Compare actual charges before and after a dated change. Second, map order, payout and supplier payment dates to understand working capital. A better margin does not itself finance the next order.

<aside class="article-example"><p class="example-label">Illustrative model, not Amazon rates</p><h3>Margin and cash can move in different directions</h3><p>A €0.20 unit fee saving across 10,000 orders yields €2,000 more contribution. In a separate hypothetical US scenario, seven additional funding days at 100 orders per day and €12 tied up in inventory represent €8,400 longer in the cash cycle. The latter is a liquidity need, not an extra expense. It does not imply a DD+7 change applies in Germany.</p></aside>

## Monthly controls

Reconcile fee previews with booked charges and cluster differences by ASIN. Review product dimensions and category classification against real product facts. Segment products after returns and advertising. Test changes to price, packaging, ads and fulfilment individually, including their demand effect.
