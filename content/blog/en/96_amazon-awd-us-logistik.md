# Amazon AWD: plan upstream inventory for a viable US supply chain

Amazon AWD can provide an upstream storage and distribution option for brands operating in the United States. The question is whether the additional inventory stage improves total economics and reliable availability. A lower storage rate alone does not answer it.

This guide concerns Amazon's US offering. It does not transfer US prices or programme conditions to Germany. Import, product requirements and financing the longer supply chain need their own assessment.

## Separate upstream stock from sellable stock

FBA supports customer-order fulfilment. AWD can hold upstream inventory and support replenishment. Goods in an upstream location are not automatically immediately available as sellable FBA inventory.

Report supplier stock, goods in transit, upstream stock, transfers and sellable FBA stock separately. Count each unit once. Advertising and sales planning must consider when units will be available at the required stage, not merely the total number owned.

## Compare the complete route

For the same goods and period, compare transport, receiving, storage duration, handling, transfers and exception costs. Confirm which services are included in current terms to avoid counting a fee twice or omitting a material cost.

<aside class="article-example"><p class="example-label">Own model · not Amazon tariffs</p><h3>A $70 advantage needs operational context</h3><p>A hypothetical existing route costs $600 storage, $200 onward transport and $100 handling, totalling $900. An illustrative AWD scenario costs $300, $350 and $180, totalling $830. The difference is $70, with common costs excluded. Extra exceptions or longer storage can reverse the comparison.</p></aside>

Run both slower and faster sell-through scenarios. Long dwell times increase the importance of storage; frequent movements can emphasise other charges. Separate one-time setup from recurring expenditure.

## Model the replenishment requirement

<aside class="article-example"><p class="example-label">Planning example · not the AWD algorithm</p><h3>Relate inventory to time and demand</h3><p>At an assumed 30 daily sales, 14 days replenishment time and seven buffer days, a simplified planning horizon represents 630 units. Actual stock, transfers, demand variation and available controls must be considered separately.</p></aside>

A large upstream holding does not prevent an FBA stockout if movement starts too late or takes longer than expected. Track actual transition times and their variation at product level.

## Validate a limited flow before expanding

Choose products based on sales stability, volume, contribution and current eligibility. Confirm carton, packaging and receiving requirements before dispatch. Correcting unsuitable preparation after shipment can add avoidable cost.

Limit the initial quantity and document handovers. Compare planned with actual time, charges and stock status. Verify that reporting captures the additional stage and that someone owns exceptions.

Set explicit conditions for expanding or reducing volume.
