# Amazon badges: how labels like Amazon's Choice and Best Seller lift your sales

In the search results, a product with a small coloured badge stands out immediately. "Best Seller", "Amazon's Choice", "Amazon recommends": these labels are more than decoration. They are trust signals that can noticeably raise click-through rate and conversion, because they give the buyer reassurance. This article explains the most important badges, how you get them and how to use them strategically.

## Why badges work

Buyers often decide in seconds and reach for shortcuts while doing so. A badge is exactly that: a fast signal saying "this product is a safe choice". It reduces the uncertainty they feel and lifts your product out of the mass. With comparable offers in particular, a badge can tip the decision. The effect lies less in the badge itself than in what it does to clicking and buying behaviour.

## The most important badges

- **Best Seller:** goes to the best-selling products in a category and rests on sales volume. It updates frequently and can be won and lost, so it is tied directly to your sales performance.
- **Amazon's Choice:** given to products that are well rated, immediately shippable and attractively priced for particular search terms. It is tied to keywords, not only to the category.
- **Climate Pledge Friendly:** marks products with recognized sustainability certifications, which matters to environmentally aware buyers.
- **New Release:** highlights successful new products and is a chance for freshly launched items to gain visibility early.

## How to get badges

Most badges cannot be bought or applied for directly, because they are awarded algorithmically on the basis of performance. For the Best Seller badge, sales volume in the right category counts above all. For Amazon's Choice, good reviews, availability (ideally Prime), a competitive price and relevance to a keyword work together. Climate Pledge Friendly requires the matching certification. The common denominator: solid sales, good reviews, reliable availability and a fair price.

## Badges as a side effect of good work

The most important thought: badges are usually the result, not the goal. Whoever works toward good reviews, availability, conversion and sales gets the badges almost automatically. It is therefore more worthwhile to work on the underlying factors than to chase the badge itself. Choosing a fitting, not overcrowded category (see category positioning) also raises the chance of a Best Seller badge.

## What not to do

Avoid tricks to force badges, such as artificial sales spikes through manipulated orders or fake reviews. Those methods break Amazon's guidelines and can get you suspended. A badge resting on manipulated signals is not stable anyway. Bet on real performance.

## In short

Amazon badges are effective trust signals that can raise click-through rate and conversion. But you do not get them through tricks, you get them through what counts anyway: good reviews, reliable availability, a fair price and solid sales in the right category. Whoever works on those foundations collects badges as a deserved side effect, and uses them to stand out in the search results.
