# Negative keywords in Amazon PPC: stopping wasted spend systematically

Anyone running ads on Amazon pays for clicks, including the wrong ones. That is where negative keywords come in. They are one of the most effective and at the same time most neglected tools in PPC. Used properly they cut off unprofitable searches and steer your budget where it sells. This article explains how they work and how to turn them into a simple routine.

## What negative keywords are

A negative keyword is a search you do **not** want your ad to appear on. Where normal keywords say "show my ad on this search", negative keywords say "block my ad here". That stops your budget going on clicks that never suited your product in the first place.

A classic example: you sell high-quality stainless steel water bottles. Through a broad campaign your ad also appears on "water bottle kids plastic". Those clicks cost money and barely convert. Add "kids" and "plastic" as negative keywords and the waste stops.

## The two match types

Negative exact excludes the exact phrase and close variations; it is not a strictly character-for-character filter. Negative phrase can exclude a broader family containing the phrase. Check the impact on relevant longer searches before applying broad exclusions. [Amazon defines the match types](https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GHTRFDZRJPW6764R).

## How to find candidates

Separate clear product mismatch from uncertain performance. Irrelevant terms can be excluded on relevance grounds. Performance decisions need settled attribution, expected conversion and an explicit spend limit. There is no universal rule to stop after 10, 15 or 30 clicks.

## Negative keywords as campaign steering

Move proven search terms only when the destination campaign actually delivers and has sufficient budget. Negating them in the original campaign too soon can remove useful reach. Separation improves budget ownership and interpretation; overlapping campaigns do not automatically bid up their own CPC.

## Common mistakes

The most common mistake is overzealousness: excluding too early and too broadly. Whoever blocks whole phrases after a handful of clicks often cuts off profitable searches before they could show themselves. The second mistake is the opposite, never tidying up at all. Maintaining negatives is not a one-off job. The third: reaching for negative phrase casually and cutting off relevant long-tail searches with it.

## A simple routine

Turn the maintenance into a fixed habit. Every one to two weeks you open the search term report, filter for clicks without sales and for extreme ACoS, and add negatives deliberately. Note what you exclude, so you recognize patterns over time. That way you build a clean, efficient account step by step, one where your budget consistently flows into searches that convert.

## In short

Negative keywords are not a side show but one of the most effective measures for efficient Amazon advertising. They stop wasted spend, sharpen your campaign structure and improve your ACoS, provided you work from data, patiently and regularly. Whoever makes the search term report a fixed routine gives away considerably less budget than the competition.
