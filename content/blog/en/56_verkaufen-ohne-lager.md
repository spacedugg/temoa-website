# Selling on Amazon without a warehouse: which models actually work

The idea is tempting: sell on Amazon without stacking boxes in the living room or renting a warehouse. There genuinely are several routes to selling without storage of your own, and they differ sharply in effort, risk and compliance. This article sorts the main models and shows what to watch for.

## Model 1: FBA, Amazon is your warehouse

The obvious route is Fulfilment by Amazon. You own the goods but do not store them yourself. You ship them to Amazon's fulfilment centres, and Amazon handles storage, shipping, customer service and returns. So you need no warehouse and still get all the advantages (Prime, scalability), in exchange for fees. Strictly speaking you are not selling without inventory but without storing it yourself. For most people this is the most workable "warehouse-free" route.

## Model 2: dropshipping, under strict rules

In dropshipping you sell products that a supplier ships directly to the customer only after the order comes in. You hold no stock. Important: Amazon permits dropshipping only under strict conditions. The central rule is that **you** must be the identifiable seller. Only your name may appear on the packing slip, the packaging and every document, not another retailer's. The classic route of having another online shop ship straight to the customer breaches Amazon's dropshipping policy and can lead to suspension.

## Model 3: print on demand

With print on demand, products (T-shirts, mugs, books) are produced individually and shipped only once ordered. Amazon runs its own programmes for this, for merchandise and for books through Kindle Direct Publishing. You supply the design, and production and shipping run automatically. That is a genuinely inventory-free model, with thin margins and a limited product range.

## What they have in common: no warehouse does not mean no work

All of these models spare you the physical warehouse, and none of them makes the business effortless. You still have to choose products, optimise listings, steer advertising, deliver customer service and calculate margins. The myth of passive income without work survives none of these models. Going warehouse-free shifts the work, it does not remove it.

## Take the rules seriously

Dropshipping in particular calls for caution, because many of the guides in circulation breach Amazon's policies. Have products shipped to Amazon customers straight from another retail shop and you risk suspension. Before starting an inventory-free model, read the current policies (dropshipping above all) closely and make sure you appear as the sole seller and control the supply chain.

## Which model for whom?

FBA suits anyone who wants a scalable business with their own (outsourced) goods and can carry the up-front financing. Compliant dropshipping suits newcomers with little capital who have a reliable, controlled supply source and can handle the margin and service side. Print on demand is ideal for creative sellers with designs who do not want inventory risk.

## Conclusion

Selling without a warehouse of your own is possible on Amazon: through FBA (outsourced storage), compliant dropshipping (you as the sole seller controlling the supply chain) or print on demand (production on order). None of these is effortless, and with dropshipping especially, compliance with Amazon's rules decides between success and suspension. Pick the model that fits your capital, goals and appetite for risk, work within the rules, and you really can build a functioning business without a warehouse.
