# More Amazon reviews, within the rules: which routes work (and which no longer exist)

Reviews are one of the strongest conversion drivers on Amazon and, at launch, the biggest bottleneck. Many sellers look for shortcuts and run into outdated advice or risky methods. Programmes such as the old Early Reviewer Program no longer exist, and much of what once circulated is now prohibited. This article covers the routes that are permitted today and actually work.

## Why reviews matter so much

The number and average of your reviews directly influence whether buyers trust you enough to order. A product with few or no reviews reads as risky, even with a strong listing. Reviews also work indirectly on ranking, because they lift conversion. Freshly launched products sit in the classic bind: no reviews without sales, and sales are hard without reviews.

## What no longer works

For clarity, because so much outdated advice circulates: the **Early Reviewer Program** was discontinued by Amazon and is no longer an option. Also prohibited and risky: bought reviews, reviews in exchange for free products or discounts with an expectation of stars, asking only for *positive* reviews, reviews from your own circle (family, friends, staff) and manipulation of any kind. Those methods can cost you every review on the listing, or the account.

## The permitted routes today

Several legitimate, effective routes exist:

- **The "Request a Review" function:** Amazon provides a standardised button that sends a neutral, compliant review request to the buyer. Because Amazon writes the message, there is nothing you can get wrong. It can also be automated through permitted tools.
- **Amazon Vine:** an official programme in which you give products to selected trusted reviewers who write honest, not guaranteed positive, reviews. Ideal for new products with few reviews. It usually assumes brand registration and can come with costs and quotas.
- **A good product and service experience:** the most durable route. A product that delivers what the listing promises, plus fast and friendly service, generates positive reviews organically and reduces negative ones.
- **Package inserts, kept neutral:** an insert may *remind* a customer about leaving a review as long as it is neutral and does not push for positive stars or offer anything in return. Care and knowledge of the rules are needed here.

## The right sequence at launch

At launch you combine these sensibly. Vine brings early credible reviews while the first sales get going. In parallel the automated Request a Review function makes sure every genuine buyer receives a neutral request. As sales volume grows, that becomes the reliable, continuous source of new reviews.

## Expectations and patience

Reviews grow slowly. Typically only a small share of buyers leaves one. Aim for steadiness rather than fast spikes. Sudden unnatural waves of reviews look suspicious and can draw Amazon's attention. Even, organic growth is not only safer, it also reads as more credible.

## Conclusion

The route to more reviews today runs through the permitted tools rather than tricks: the neutral Request a Review function, the Vine programme for the start and, above all, a good product with good service. Outdated or manipulative methods, the discontinued Early Reviewer Program included, are no longer an option and carry real risk. Build reviews compliantly, patiently and steadily and you strengthen conversion and ranking for the long run without putting the account at risk.
