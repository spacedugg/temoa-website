# Getting a negative Amazon review removed: what really works and what does not

A bad review stings, especially when it is unfair or does not even describe your product. Many sellers then ask: can I have this review removed? The honest answer is sometimes yes, often no. What decides it is whether the review breaches Amazon's policies. This article explains which reviews can be removed, how to go about it correctly and how to handle criticism that is justified.

## The distinction that matters: product review vs. seller feedback

First a clarification many people miss. There are two kinds of feedback:

- **Product reviews:** about the product itself (stars and text on the product page).
- **Seller feedback:** about your performance as a seller (shipping, service).

Seller feedback that actually concerns the *product*, or that concerns FBA fulfilment, can often be removed because it sits in the wrong place. For product reviews the bar is higher.

## When a review can be removed

Amazon removes reviews only when they breach the community guidelines, not because they are negative. Typical breaches that justify removal:

- **Insults, hate speech or inappropriate language.**
- **Reviews containing private or personal data.**
- **Obvious spam or advertising**, for example pointing to other products or sellers.
- **Reviews that are not about the product**, such as pure shipping complaints on an FBA item, or a mix-up with a different product.
- **Manipulated or paid reviews**, including negative ones placed by competitors.
- **Conflicts of interest**, for example reviews from competitors or from people close to them.

Fair, factual criticism of the product meets none of these, and it stays up.

## How to report a review that breaks the rules

If you suspect a genuine breach, be factual about it. Report the review through the "report abuse" function and through seller support, and explain specifically *which policy* it breaches, pointing to the passage in question. The more precisely you name the breach, the better your chance of removal. A plain "this review is unfair, please delete it" rarely succeeds.

## What you must never do

Never try to get a bad review removed by contacting the buyer with pressure, incentives or anything in return. That breaches the policies and can put your account at risk. Equally out of bounds: buying positive reviews to dilute negative ones. Those methods are risky, and the damage when they are found is severe.

## Handling justified criticism

Most negative reviews breach no rule at all, and that is where the real opportunity sits. Fair criticism is valuable feedback: it shows you where the product or the description falls short. Respond factually and helpfully where the format allows, fix the underlying cause, and build more positive reviews through the permitted routes. A single critical star in a sea of good reviews does little harm, and it makes the whole set read as more credible than unbroken praise.

## Conclusion

Negative reviews can be removed only when they breach Amazon's policies, not because they are uncomfortable. So check first whether a real breach exists (wrong category, insult, spam, manipulation) and then report it precisely with reference to the rule. Where the criticism is justified, the better route is to use it as feedback, fix the cause and build more genuine positive reviews. Manipulation is not an option: it endangers exactly what you are trying to protect.
