# Importing from Alibaba and selling on Amazon: the guide for newcomers

For many private label sellers the Amazon business begins with the same question: how do I get a good product? One of the most common answers is Alibaba, the large sourcing platform through which you reach manufacturers directly, mostly in Asia. Used properly it is a powerful tool. Used naively it is a source of expensive mistakes. This article walks you through the key steps.

## What Alibaba is (and is not)

Alibaba is a B2B marketplace connecting buyers with manufacturers and traders, built for wholesale rather than single orders. Do not confuse it with AliExpress, which sells individual items to consumers. On Alibaba you negotiate quantities, prices and customisation directly with suppliers, and you can have products made under your own brand.

## Step 1: find serious suppliers

Not every supplier is trustworthy. Look for signals such as verification status, years in operation, transaction history and whether you are dealing with an actual manufacturer or only a middleman. Contact several suppliers, ask specific questions (capacity, certifications, customisation options) and compare the answers. How professionally and quickly a supplier communicates is itself a quality signal.

## Step 2: order and check samples

Never order a large quantity without checking samples first. Samples show you the actual quality, expose weaknesses and form the basis for your listing photos. Ideally order samples from several suppliers so you can compare. Sample costs are a small, indispensable investment against the risk of receiving a whole batch of substandard goods.

## Step 3: negotiate and settle the terms

On Alibaba almost everything is negotiable: price, minimum order quantity, customisation, packaging and payment terms. Also settle the specifications clearly in writing: material, dimensions, colours, packaging, quality standards. The more precise the agreement, the lower the risk of misunderstandings in production.

## Step 4: pay safely

Payment security is decisive. Use protected payment routes where you can, such as Alibaba's Trade Assurance or other buyer protection mechanisms, rather than risky direct transfers to people you do not know. A deposit with the balance on or after completion is common, so negotiate terms that limit your exposure. On large orders an independent quality inspection before shipment is worth the cost.

## Step 5: shipping, duty and import costs

A frequently underestimated block is the total cost of importing: freight (sea or air), duties, import charges and transport to the Amazon warehouse. That landed cost belongs in your margin calculation without exception. A product that is cheap ex works can be unprofitable after freight and duty. Settle the delivery terms as well, in particular who carries which leg of the transport.

## Step 6: secure compliance and quality

Make sure your product meets every required standard and marking for your target market: safety, certifications, labelling. Get the relevant documents from the manufacturer. A quality inspection before shipment, ideally by an independent provider, keeps a faulty batch from surfacing at the Amazon warehouse when it is too late.

## Conclusion

Importing through Alibaba is a proven route to your own Amazon product, as long as you go about it methodically: vet suppliers, order samples, negotiate clearly, pay safely, count every import cost and secure quality and compliance. The most common expensive mistakes, substandard goods, nasty surprises at customs and freight, unclear specifications, are all avoidable. Take the time for careful sourcing and you lay the foundation for a profitable private label product.
