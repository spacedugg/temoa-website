# Amazon Attribution: making external traffic measurable and using it deliberately

Many brands send traffic to their Amazon listings from outside, through Google Ads, Instagram, TikTok, influencers or their own newsletter. The problem: as soon as the click lands on Amazon, the trail disappears. Nobody knows which source actually sold. Amazon Attribution closes exactly that gap. This article explains what the tool is, why it matters and how to use it.

## The problem with external traffic

Anyone investing in external channels wants to know whether it pays off. But Amazon is a closed world: a click from an Instagram story and a click from a Google ad look identical on your detail page. Without measurement you are in the dark, knowing that something is happening overall but not which channel actually drives the revenue. Budget then flows on instinct rather than on effect.

## What Amazon Attribution is

Amazon Attribution is a free measurement tool for brand-registered sellers. It generates special tracking links for your external campaigns. When somebody clicks such a link through to your listing, Amazon attributes what follows to that source, and shows you metrics such as clicks, detail page views, add-to-cart actions and actual purchases per channel.

## Why that is strategically valuable

With Attribution you can finally treat external channels like performance marketing: measure, compare, optimize. You see whether an influencer really brings purchases or only reach, whether Google Ads pay off on your product, and which social channel delivers the best conversion. That lets you move budget deliberately to where it produces revenue.

There is a second effect. External traffic that converts sends positive signals to Amazon's algorithm and can support your organic ranking. Amazon has even rewarded this in the past through the "brand referral bonus", a rebate on externally driven sales. Whether and in what form such programmes currently apply is worth checking in your account, because the terms can change.

## How to go about it

The process is manageable. In Amazon Attribution you set up a campaign, define source and channel and generate the tracking links. You then use those links in your external ads, posts or newsletters instead of the normal Amazon URL. After that you watch the report to see which source delivers which results. Clean, consistent naming of your campaigns matters, so the reporting stays readable.

## Common mistakes

The first mistake is sending external traffic without attribution links, which leaves you with no basis for measurement at all. The second is judging too early, because external channels often need more data points before a pattern shows. The third is looking only at the last click and underestimating the role channels play in early brand awareness. And finally: sending external traffic to a weak listing. The same rule applies here, build conversion strength first, then buy traffic.

## In short

Amazon Attribution turns external traffic from a black box into a measurable, steerable channel. Anyone investing in Google, social or influencers should use the tool to see what actually sells and shift budget accordingly. As a free tool for brand-registered sellers it is one of the simplest ways to run off-Amazon marketing on facts instead of on hunches.
