# Amazon Small and Light is gone: what the change means for cheap, light products

> **Market note:** this article describes the programme change mainly **in the US market**. Europe had a comparable programme whose benefits were likewise folded into cheaper rates for low-price products. Check the FBA fees currently in force for your European marketplaces (DE, FR, NL, ES, IT).

Anyone selling small, light, low-price products through FBA knew the Small and Light programme: reduced shipping fees for cheap lightweights in exchange for slightly longer delivery times. That programme in its original form was discontinued by Amazon and folded into the regular rate structures. This article explains what that means and how to handle it as a seller.

## What Small and Light was

Small and Light was a special FBA programme for low-price, small and light items. It offered considerably reduced fulfilment fees, because such products were often barely profitable to ship at normal FBA rates. The trade-off was slightly slower delivery than standard Prime shipping. For many sellers of small parts, consumables and accessories it was an important way to rescue thin margins.

## What changed

Amazon ended the standalone Small and Light programme and integrated its logic into the regular FBA fee structure, among other things through cheaper rates for low-price products inside the normal FBA system. The basic idea, reduced fees for cheap lightweights, therefore lives on, just not as a separate programme with its own enrolment and delivery-time rules. Since Amazon adjusts fees and programme structures continuously, check the current FBA rates with Amazon directly.

## What that means in practice

For most sellers of small, cheap products the good news is that reduced fees for such items still exist, now automatically inside regular FBA, without the administrative overhead of a separate programme and without the slower delivery times Small and Light brought with it. That can improve delivery times while keeping the cost advantage. In detail, though, the specific rates can differ from the old Small and Light terms.

## What to do now

Concretely:

- **Recalculate the fees:** check the current FBA fees for your cheap lightweights and compare them with your previous calculation. Your margin may have moved, up or down.
- **Review prices and range:** if individual products become unprofitable under the changed fees, look at price adjustments, bundling or whether FBM is the cheaper alternative.
- **Optimise packaging:** size and weight decide the fee tier, and every reduction can move you into a cheaper one.

## The larger lesson

The end of Small and Light illustrates an important truth about the Amazon business: programmes, fees and rules change constantly. Build your calculation on a single special programme and you are exposed when it disappears. Robust businesses recalculate their margin regularly and do not rest on one concession.

## Conclusion

Small and Light as a standalone programme is history, while the idea of reduced fees for cheap lightweights carried over into regular FBA, in some cases with better delivery times. For sellers of such products that mainly means looking up the current fees, recalculating the margin and optimising packaging. And the wider lesson: on Amazon a great deal changes continuously, and anyone who recalculates regularly stays on the safe side.
