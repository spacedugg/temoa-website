# Amazon and AI visibility: make product facts clear and consistent

Amazon and AI visibility begins with a product that can be described accurately. Conflicting dimensions, incomplete compatibility and unsupported claims create uncertainty for customers and for systems that interpret product information. Consistency is a practical starting point, not a promise of a particular AI ranking.

Separate Amazon product fields from your own website and from external search or assistant systems. Their access, inputs and reporting differ. A website recommendation from Google cannot automatically be treated as a requirement for every Amazon feature.

## Establish an approved product fact record

Maintain a source for each important claim and name the person who can approve changes. Connect the record to the actual model and variation. Marketing copy should be derived from verified information rather than becoming a competing source of truth.

| Information | Evidence to maintain |
|---|---|
| Dimensions and materials | Approved product specification |
| Compatibility | Supported models and explicit exclusions |
| Package contents | Actual delivered configuration |
| Use conditions | Instructions and documented limits |
| Performance claims | Appropriate supporting evidence |

## Resolve contradictions before adding more text

<aside class="article-example"><p class="example-label">Illustrative compatibility audit</p><h3>One approved list replaces three conflicting claims</h3><p>A product page lists six supported models, an image lists eight and an old website page names a discontinued range. The product team confirms seven supported models and two explicit exclusions. The task is to update each surface from that verified record, then check the live result. Adding a broad claim such as “works with all models” would increase uncertainty.</p></aside>

Prioritise facts that affect purchase suitability. A clear exclusion can prevent unsuitable demand and make the product easier to understand. Do not fabricate answers to fill an imagined content requirement.

## Keep website SEO requirements in scope

Google's guidance for its AI search features does not introduce a separate special technical requirement or special AI schema for inclusion. Existing search eligibility and useful, accessible content remain relevant. This statement concerns Google and should not be generalised to all assistant systems.

For your website, check crawlable content, internal links and accurate structured data where applicable. For Amazon, work within available product attributes and current content rules. Neither approach guarantees citation, display or traffic.

## Monitor precise questions

Create a short set of commercially relevant questions about suitability, differences and limitations. Record what a system displays, when it was checked and which source appears to support the answer. Outputs can vary, so isolated observations should not become definitive ranking claims.

Changes in branded search or sales do not by themselves prove that an AI system caused demand. Keep visibility observations separate from causal performance statements.
