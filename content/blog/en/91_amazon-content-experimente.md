# Amazon content experiments: turn a hypothesis into a commercial decision

Amazon content experiments should test a customer decision, not merely whether a redesign looks newer. Established brands benefit when a specific uncertainty connects to a measurable outcome: product fit, compatibility, included components or the difference between variants.

Correct factual errors immediately. A test must not knowingly retain an inaccurate claim simply to preserve a control. Experimental design starts once both versions are accurate and acceptable.

## Choose a hypothesis with a plausible mechanism

For example: a dimensions image may reduce uncertainty about whether a product fits. State what changes and why it could influence purchase behaviour. Testing several connected changes can assess the complete package, but it cannot isolate the contribution of each element.

| Customer uncertainty | Possible content task |
|---|---|
| Will it fit? | Clear dimensions and installation context |
| What is included? | Accurate package-content image |
| Which variant do I need? | Verifiable variant comparison |
| How is it used? | Concise usage sequence |

Check current Manage Your Experiments eligibility for the account, ASIN and content type. Access should be verified before committing to a testing calendar.

## Connect the possible lift to economics

<aside class="article-example"><p class="example-label">Illustrative business case</p><h3>A small change can matter at sufficient volume</h3><p>At 20,000 monthly sessions, a hypothetical conversion change from 10% to 10.5% means 100 additional orders if other assumptions hold. At €9 contribution per order, that is €900 per month. A €1,800 production cost would be recovered in two months under those sustained assumptions. The change is 0.5 percentage points, or 5% relative, not five percentage points.</p></aside>

This calculation is not evidence that the lift will occur. Use it to determine whether the potential improvement is commercially meaningful and what uncertainty the decision can tolerate.

## Decide interpretation rules before launch

Record the primary outcome, expected observation period, material interruptions and decision owner. Avoid declaring a winner after a promising early fluctuation. Repeatedly stopping when a favourable result appears can distort interpretation.

Check stock, price and other changes during the experiment. A result from one product or season may not transfer to the rest of the catalogue. Preserve uncertainty instead of reporting a precise uplift without context.

## Use all three possible outcomes

A credible improvement may justify rollout and further testing. A credible deterioration can expose a mistaken assumption. An inconclusive result may mean the effect is small, the evidence is insufficient or the variants do not meaningfully differ.

Archive the hypothesis, assets, dates, operational changes, outcome and decision. This prevents future teams from repeating the same test without learning from it. [Search Query Performance](/blog/search-query-performance) and customer questions can supply the next hypothesis.
