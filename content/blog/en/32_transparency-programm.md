# Amazon Transparency: how to protect your brand from counterfeits

Counterfeits are a permanent threat to successful brands on Amazon. They undercut your price, damage your reputation through poor quality and cost you revenue. Brand Registry gives you a first set of protection tools, but if product piracy is a serious problem in your category, the Transparency programme goes a decisive step further. This article explains how it works and who it pays off for.

## What Transparency is

Transparency is a product authentication programme run by Amazon. Every single unit of your product carries a unique individual code (a Transparency code) applied during manufacture or packing. Amazon checks that code before an item ships to a customer, so that only genuine units are dispatched. Customers can also scan the code themselves in the app and verify authenticity.

## How it differs from Brand Registry

Brand Registry gives you tools to *report* infringements after they happen. Transparency starts earlier: it is meant to keep counterfeit units out of the dispatch process in the first place by making every genuine unit individually identifiable. That makes it proactive, unit-level protection rather than a reactive reporting route. The two complement each other, and Transparency usually assumes an existing Brand Registry.

## How it works day to day

The process runs in several steps. You enrol your products (by GTIN or barcode) in Transparency, receive the unique codes and apply them to every unit, either directly in production at the manufacturer or during packing. From then on those codes are part of the supply chain. Amazon checks them at dispatch, and customers can scan them in the app to see authenticity and, in some cases, extra product information.

## Who it pays off for

Transparency is not a must for everyone. It is particularly valuable when:

- your category is heavily affected by counterfeits,
- your brand is established enough to attract imitators,
- an authentic, trust-building customer experience matters to you,
- you are willing to carry the extra effort in production and packing.

For very young products or ones nobody bothers to copy, the effort can outweigh the benefit.

## Judging effort and cost realistically

Transparency adds operational work. Codes have to reach every unit reliably, which takes coordination with the manufacturer and clean processes. There can also be a per-code cost. Weigh that effort against the potential damage counterfeits do. For a brand attacked by pirates on a regular basis the protection is usually worth the investment. For a small niche with no counterfeit problem it usually is not.

## One element of a protection concept

Transparency is powerful, but it does not do everything. It works best as part of a whole: Brand Registry as the foundation and reporting tool, Transparency for unit-level authentication, and consistent monitoring of your listings for unauthorised sellers. Protection becomes robust only when those pieces work together.

## Conclusion

Amazon Transparency turns every unit into a verifiable original and stops counterfeits before they reach the customer. For established brands in counterfeit-prone categories it is strong proactive protection, and it comes with extra effort and possible cost. If you have a real counterfeit problem, look at it seriously as part of a layered protection concept. If you do not have one yet, start with Brand Registry and add to it later.
